**Edit ▸ Search & Replace…** (Ctrl+F) opens a panel over the text. Type what you are looking for and the matches are
marked in the paragraphs as you type; Enter goes to the next match, Shift+Enter to the previous one. If words are
selected when you open it, they are the first thing it looks for. Ctrl+H opens the same panel with **Replace with**
already showing.

## Where it looks

The **Look in** button says what is searched: **Everything (5)**, or the names of the kinds that are on. Press it
for a list with a checkbox for each kind and the number of matches it has: **Text** (body paragraphs), **Headings**,
**Remarks**, **Objects** (the words in text boxes and shapes that sit on the text) and **Descriptions** (the
descriptions that are read aloud for an object). **All** and **None** at the bottom of the list switch every kind on
or off. The list beside the button says how far to look: **The whole text**, **This section** or **Selection**; the
checkboxes **Match case** and **Whole text** narrow it further. A match in an object is not drawn in the text: the list names it, and a click
takes you to the paragraph the object belongs to.

## Moving the panel

Drag the panel by its title bar to put it where it does not cover what you are working on. It stays where you leave it
until you reload the page. From the keyboard, **Alt+arrow keys** move it (**Alt+Shift+arrow keys** in bigger steps;
Option on a Mac), unless the cursor is in a text field, where those keys keep their own meaning. Every dialog moves the
same way.

## Describing more than a word

**Build a pattern…** puts a search together from parts — "a word", "a letter, then one or more digits" — and a
sentence under them says back what is being found. **Only in texts that…** adds a demand on the whole text. Texts that
match the parts but fail a demand are listed under **Left out**, with the reason.

## Replacing

Switch on **Replace with…** and each match shows what it would become. Untick the ones to keep, then press
**Replace this one** or **Replace all**. All of it is one step in the history, so Undo brings every text back. The new
words are yours, and the words around them keep their authors. A paragraph in which someone else is writing, or that
was typed into after you looked, is left as it is and named.
