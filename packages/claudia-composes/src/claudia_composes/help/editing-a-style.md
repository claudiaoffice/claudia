Every paragraph has a style — Body text, Heading 1, Quote, and so on — that says what it is *for*. Format ▸ "Edit
this style…" (or Shift+F10, or right-click) opens a gallery of every style in the document, with the look of
whichever one is open below it: colour, font, size, bold, alignment, or the style's CSS directly.

Clicking a style in the gallery only shows what it looks like — it never changes your paragraph. The style that is
really under your cursor is marked "here"; click any other row to look at (or edit) a different style without
touching your text.

## Where a change goes

Two choices sit above the controls:

- **Every use of "X"** changes the style itself, everywhere it is used in the document.
- **This paragraph only** takes your paragraph out of the style and gives it its own look, under a provisional
  name such as "Body text (changed)" that you can rename or leave as is.

"This paragraph only" only makes sense for the style your cursor is actually in — there is no paragraph to split
off from a style you are merely looking at, so that choice is unavailable while you are browsing another style's
look, and comes back the moment you return to your own paragraph's style (or close the menu and reopen it there).

## Example

To make every heading in the document bold: put your cursor in a line styled Heading 1, open Format ▸ "Edit this
style…", choose "Every use of 'Heading 1'", and press the Bold button. Every Heading 1 paragraph changes at once —
nothing is copied, and no new style is created.

To set off one particular paragraph without touching the style it is based on: with your cursor in it, leave the
default "This paragraph only" and make your change; it becomes its own style, based on the one you started from.

## All styles…

Format ▸ "All styles…" lists every style in the document with how many paragraphs use it, and lets you rename one
that is still waiting for a name.
