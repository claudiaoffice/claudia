Every document can have one AI user, shown in the toolbox's chat like any other person. Type `@ai` followed by
what you want, the same way you would write `@maria` to get a colleague's attention.

The assistant only *suggests* — it has no way to change your text itself. Each suggestion is shown inline where it
applies; accept it to make the change, or leave it and it stays open for later.

## What it understands

- `@ai review this text` (or `check`, `proofread`, `edit`, `improve`, `polish`, `tighten`, `fix`, `look`) reads the
  whole document and suggests fixes for spelling, wordy phrases, "very", doubled spaces, and flags sentences that
  have grown very long.
- `@ai check this paragraph` (or `here`, or mention "the cursor") narrows the same review to wherever your own
  cursor currently is, instead of the whole document.
- `@ai help` (or `hi`, `hello`, `hey`, or asking what it can do) answers with a short reminder of what it
  understands.
- Anything else gets an honest "I did not understand that" rather than a guess.

## Example

Write a paragraph with a typo and a long, wandering sentence, then send `@ai review this text`. The assistant
answers in chat with how many suggestions it made, and each one appears attached to the exact words it is about —
a strikethrough-and-replacement for a wording fix, or a remark alone for something like a long sentence, which it
cannot fix for you. Nothing in your document changes until you accept a suggestion.

## Why it only suggests

Claudia's AI user edits through the very same methods a person does, so it can never do something a person
couldn't also undo — accepting or rejecting its suggestions is just you, deciding what gets written, with its
read of the text as a second opinion rather than a silent edit.
