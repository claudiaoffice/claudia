# D210 · A colour is picked by what it means; a colour of its own is one step further in, and is told what it costs

**A colour is picked by what it means; a colour of its own is one step further in, and is told what it costs.** The picker lists the document's roles by name and purpose, with how strong a wash for a fill (`@accent/14`). "Off the palette…" opens the hex field and the suite's swatches, and a literal colour then reads as a deviation: it says a palette change will leave it behind, and offers the nearest role in one click. Never blocked (D202). The readability note is in plain words — "The words will be hard to read on this background" — and no ratio is ever shown to a reader. The palette dialog shows each role with its purpose and how many styles name it, what it is read against as *Easy to read* / *Hard to read*, what a change would cost **before** it is made, and which styles will move with it.

**Notes.** Straight from the four artboards the owner agreed, with one correction the code forced: the palette a document starts with now comes from the server's own `palette` answer (`builtin`) instead of a copy of the nine roles in the client. A second list of the same nine colours in TypeScript would have been a thing to keep in step, and the one that drifts is always the copy.
