As an operator, I want commands grouped into compressed, encrypted blocks, so that the file stays small and redaction is cheap.

**Acceptance:** a block size policy; appending without rewriting old blocks (relaxed to a size trigger, D1269); the compression ratio measured against per-command encryption on real logs.

**Status evidence:** blocks are built (about 1 MiB each, framed, zlib-compressed, sealed, chained by hash). **Not done:** the size policy as a stated decision, the measurement against per-command encryption, and appending without rewriting — packing writes the whole zip again.

**Decided 2026-11-19.** The block policy is written down: 1 MiB before compression, closed blocks are not changed except by redaction, key renewal and deleting a private domain, the last open block is replaced as it grows (D1269). The measurement against per-command sealing is part of this round. "Appending without rewriting old blocks" in the acceptance is relaxed to a size trigger of about 50 MB (PERF-06).

**Measured 2026-11-19 (slice B of the second round).** Block compression against sealing every command on its own (the
command's bytes plus 28 bytes of nonce and tag, no compression across commands), on a log made through the real server:
computes, one `set_cells` per change, a mix of numbers, formulas and short text, cycling through 40 rows × 6 columns, sealed
with the history key.

| Changes | Raw bytes (JSON of each) | One by one | In blocks | Ratio | Blocks |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 43 089 | 48 717 | 4 064 | 12.0 | 1 |
| 2 000 | 433 607 | 489 635 | 36 737 | 13.3 | 1 |
| 6 000 | 1 305 138 | 1 473 166 | 109 023 | 13.5 | 2 |

About 12–13 times smaller than sealing each change on its own, and about 18 bytes a change in a block. **Caveat, and an open
point:** these logs are made of typing, not the "real logs of the test documents" the acceptance asks for: the test documents
are office files, and importing one makes a log of a single step. A log from real use (several people, several apps, undo,
pasting, a table) should be measured when one exists; until then the ratio above is the evidence and it is an upper bound
on typing-like logs, not a promise for every document. The measurement was a throw-away script and is not kept in the
repository; a repeat needs a longer log from the same method.

**Done, 2026-11-22 (branch `e2-5-close`).** D1269's promise is pinned by a test: a closed block keeps its bytes when
later changes are packed, and only the last, still small block is sealed again
(`test_a_closed_block_keeps_its_bytes_when_later_changes_are_packed`). The measurement is kept as
`scripts/measure_blocks.py`: `--typing 200 2000 6000` makes the typing log again, `--document FILE --name NAME` measures
the history of a real document, both through the real packing code. Its typing log gives 15.4, 17.3 and 17.6 (the values
typed differ from the lost script of 2026-11-19, which gave the table above). **Follow-up, not part of this story:**
measure a log from real use (several people, several apps, undo, pasting, a table) with `--document` once one exists.
