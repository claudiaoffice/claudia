How styles work in claudia

A note on styles, purposes, facets, style kinds and slots, from the glossary session of 21 November 2026. The words used here are defined in claudiaoffice-log/glossary.md.

In claudia a style says what something is for. Everything that serves the same purpose looks the same, because it carries the same style. Nobody makes one word bold “just here”: whoever wants another look picks, or makes, a style with another purpose.

That is the whole idea (D306). The rest of this note explains how claudia makes it work across a text, a spreadsheet and a slide deck: which parts of a document can carry a style, what a style is made of, and why some styles fit some places and not others.

The words

Style
A named look, defined by its purpose, put into slots. Every slot with the same style serves the same purpose. Example: Quote, “the words of other people”.
Purpose
What something in a document is for. A style states it in a sentence.
Facet
One of four sides of a look, naming what can be set for it: text (how characters look), paragraph (how a stretch of text is laid out), box (background, border and padding around content) and outline (a shape’s paint). The values are always a style’s own.
Style kind
Which facets a style may hold, and so which slots it fits: a paragraph style (text, paragraph and box), a box style or an outline style.
Slot
A part of a document that always holds exactly one style: a paragraph, the words of a cell, the box of a text box. A thing may have several slots, each with its own purpose.
Thing
Anything in a document with a name, unique in that document, that can be referenced.
Palette colour
A colour named by what it means (accent, muted); every style that uses it follows when it changes.
Number format
How the value of a cell reads (a percentage, euros, a date). It belongs to the cell, not to its style.

A text box, step by step

A text box is a thing with two slots: its box (the rectangle: background, border, padding) and its words (what the text in it looks like). Each slot holds one style. Here the box holds Warning and the words hold Quote.

Four things to take from the picture:

  • Facets are the drawers inside a style. The values (red border, italic serif 11 pt, an indent of 1 cm) sit in the style’s drawers and belong to that style.
  • The kind says which drawers a style may have. A box style has only a box drawer; a paragraph style may have text, paragraph and box drawers; an outline style has only an outline drawer.
  • A slot reads only the drawers it understands and ignores the rest.
  • A slot is never empty. It always holds exactly one style; a cell nobody styled falls back to its sheet’s default.

A paper form, for comparison

A facet is a section of a form with its fields printed on it: Border: ___  Fill: ___. The section is the same for every style. A style is one filled-in copy of the form. CSS, and SVG for a shape’s paint, are only the handwriting in which the values are written down: notation, not the substance of either the facet or the style.

Which style fits which slot

A style fits a slot when its kind holds every facet the slot reads (D316). So:

  • A words slot reads text and paragraph, so it takes only paragraph styles.
  • A box slot reads box, so it takes box styles and paragraph styles, since a paragraph style may hold a box too. That is how one style can dress a whole cell: put Input into both of a cell’s slots, and its box gives the cell’s background and border while its text and paragraph give the words.
  • An outline slot takes only outline styles.
  • A picture’s box reads nothing of a style yet, and takes only box styles.
  • A style and the style it is based on are always of the same kind.

Slides, sheets, chart tabs and named ranges are things without a slot today; a slide’s background or a page may become slots later (D315). Cells have slots but are not things. Things and slots overlap, and neither contains the other.

What a style is not

Not one per thing

A style goes into a slot, not onto a whole thing. Take a text box styled Warning whose words are a Quote. With one style per thing you would need a style “Warning containing a quote”, and soon one for every combination. With one style per slot it is two choices, each with a clear purpose: the box warns, the words quote.

Not a number format

A cell has a style and a number format, and neither decides the other (D1410). A cell styled Quote may show a percentage; otherwise there would have to be a style “the one I use when I quote a percentage”, which makes no sense. The style says what the cell is for and how it looks; the number format says how its value reads. Setting a number format is therefore not direct formatting.

Not direct formatting

You cannot click on a cell or a word and say “leave it as it is, but make it bold”. If someone really insists, that makes a new style, with a purpose of its own. The goal is a clean style language in a document, in which a person does not get lost micromanaging her own style decisions (D306).

How styles share

A style holds its own values, and today they are shared in exactly two ways:

  • Based on. A style may be based on one other style of the same kind and inherit everything it does not set itself. Explanatory note based on Quote reads like a quote until Note sets something of its own.
  • Palette colours. A style may name a colour by its meaning, such as accent, not by its value. Change accent once in the palette and every style that names it follows, in all three apps.

Open question (D1412, undecided): could a drawer’s contents be shared?

Quote and Explanatory note should read alike but differ in their box; Warning has a red box. Note based on Quote gets Quote’s text, but cannot also take Warning’s box: a style has only one parent. If a facet’s values could stand on their own, a text facet “Reading text” could be named and used by Quote and Note alike, and changed once for both.

Against it, for now: a second set of named things to manage, where D306 asks for a small, clean style language. The likelier next step is a palette of type, named fonts as palette colours are named colours, which would cover the most common sharing with less to manage.
