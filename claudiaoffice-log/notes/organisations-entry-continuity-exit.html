Organisations and their members: entry, continuity, exit

How a team or a company could work with claudia documents when people join, fall ill or go on leave, and leave: the atlas and steward model. Reconstructed on 21 November 2026 from sheet 7 of the conversation “File management” of 14 November 2026, in the words of glossary.md.

Not decided. As of 21 November 2026 none of this is decided. It is a proposal that was drawn and discussed once and never written down; this note only keeps it from being lost again. It has no decision number. Where a part already exists in claudia, the note says so; everything else is new work that nobody has agreed to.

Why organisations need something of their own

claudia has no accounts (D20). A person exists only inside each document, as a name and a password that belong to that document, and nobody outside a document can see who is in it (D102). That is the privacy model, and it has a price for organisations: there is no directory in which IT could switch a person on or off, and no list of the documents someone can unlock. The model below works with what a document already has (its key slots, its list of people, its history) and adds three things for organisations that want them: an atlas, a steward and a hand-over wizard.

1 · Entry: the places and the key arrive separately

Anna joins the team. Nobody gives her an account.

  1. IT shares the atlas. The organisation keeps one atlas of its places (the team server, the demo server). Anna enters the atlas code, and the places appear among hers. The atlas holds no keys and no document names, so the code may travel by e-mail. Members can read the atlas; only IT can change it.
  2. An owner adds a key slot for her. Ben, who owns the budget document, adds Anna with her name and a first password (Share…). The first password reaches her through another channel, never with the atlas. IT is not involved and cannot see which documents she joins.
  3. She unlocks and makes the password her own. At her first unlock Anna chooses her own password; Ben's first one stops working. The document records that she joined.

2 · Continuity: a steward key slot in every document

People fall ill, forget passwords and leave on bad terms. The organisation keeps one key slot of its own in each of its documents.

  • Sealed to the organisation, not to a person. The steward key slot is sealed to the organisation's public key. Whoever adds it cannot open it again; only the organisation's private key can.
  • Visible, not hidden. Everybody who can unlock the document sees the steward in its list of people. A hidden escrow would break the trust the privacy model rests on. Sheet 7 called it “the IAM model returned on purpose, for organisations that want it”; individuals, activists and small teams run without it.
  • The server can require it. A setting on the organisation's own server could refuse new documents that have no steward key slot. The server can check that the slot is there; it still cannot read anything.
  • Two custodians. The private key is split between two people and kept offline, in the organisation's safe. Every use of it is recorded in the document it unlocks.
  • What it is for. Ben leaves without handing the budget over. The custodians use the steward to unlock it, make Cara the owner, and the keys are renewed.

The steward is not the answer to holidays

Using the steward is a ceremony with two custodians and an offline key. It is a last resort for an owner who is gone for good, or on bad terms; it should not be needed because an owner is on parental leave. Everyday continuity (two people responsible for a document, so that one can be away) is a different question: whether a document may have more than one owner. That question is open as well (see the last section).

3 · Exit: hand over, remove, renew

Anna leaves the organisation. Because nobody outside a document can see who is in it, the only complete list of her documents is her own: Mine, on her client.

  1. A hand-over wizard walks her list. For each document she either names a successor (a hand-over to an editor) or leaves it. Both actions exist in every document today; the wizard only puts them in one place. The organisation can ask for this as part of leaving.
  2. Remove and renew are one action. When an owner removes her, the parts she could read get new keys, so that a copy of the file she might get hold of does not open what is written afterwards. What she has already seen or copied cannot be taken back (D20); the dialog says so.
  3. The steward finishes what she skipped, using the same two steps, and IT replaces the atlas code so that she no longer receives the organisation's places. Her own devices and her own list stay hers; they hold titles and addresses, never keys.

Open questions

  1. Is a steward key slot acceptable in claudia at all? It brings back a piece of central control, visibly and only where an organisation's server asks for it.
  2. Who holds the atlas's write key, and may it be IT alone?
  3. Is signed history a server setting, or a choice per document?
  4. May a document have more than one owner? Raised on 21 November 2026 for everyday continuity (holidays, illness, two people responsible). The document file already allows several owners; the server allows only one today. Deferred as undecided D1413 (backlog E2.13); until then new work does not assume a document has only one owner. If co-owners come, the steward stays the last resort and co-owners carry the everyday case.
